# GOAL:
Analyze the following customer feedback to generate the following fields:
• Sentiment
• Emotion
• Themes
• Priority
• Confidence
• Summary

# GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS:
- Return ONLY valid JSON.
- Do not include markdown fences.
- Do not include explanations.

# OUTPUT SCHEMA:
{
  "sentiment": "",
  "emotion": "",
  "confidence": 0,
  "priority": "",
  "themes": [],
  "summary": ""
}



# TASK 1 (Sentiment):

Be able to group customer feedback by overall sentiment to analyze trends.

# DECISION RULES:
- Extract a single sentiment label.
- Choose the sentiment that best represents the customer's dominant sentiment.
- Do not infer sentiment that is not reasonably supported by the text.
- If the feedback contains both positive and negative experiences, select
"Mixed".

# SENTIMENT LABELS:
- Positive
- Neutral
- Negative
- Mixed

# EXAMPLES:

issue in a single contact.
Output:

Output:

- Base your decision on the entire feedback, not isolated phrases.
- If the feedback is purely factual with no discernible emotion, return

emotion that is most directly supported by the wording.

# EMOTION LABELS:

- Relieved

- Frustrated

# TASK 4 (Priority):

Determine the priority level of the customer's feedback. Priority reflects how
urgently the business should respond based on the customer's experience and the
potential business impact.

# DECISION RULES:
- Evaluate the overall customer experience.
- Consider both the severity of the issue and the customer's language.
- If multiple issues are mentioned, base the priority on the most serious issue.
- Do not increase priority solely because the customer uses emotional language.
- Suggestions without significant problems should generally be Low priority.

# PRIORITY LEVELS:
High
- The customer experiences an ongoing issue that the customer feels was not
resolved.
- The customer experienced a serious issue such as unresolved complaints,
safety concerns, repeated service failures, or requests for immediate
assistance.
- The issue significantly impacts the customer's ability to use or enjoy the product or service.
- The customer expresses a strong likelihood of not returning, cancelling, or discouraging others.

Medium
- The customer experienced a noticeable problem, but it is not critical.
- The issue negatively affected the experience but is likely recoverable.
- Examples include delayed delivery, confusing assembly instructions, inaccurate website information, or average customer service.

Low
- The feedback is primarily positive.
- The customer makes a suggestion for improvement.
- The issue is minor and does not significantly impact the overall experience.

# OUTPUT:
Return exactly one of:
- High
- Medium
- Low

# EXAMPLES:
Input:
The dresser arrived broken and I still haven't received a replacement.
Output: High

Input:
Delivery was a few days late, but everything else was fine.
Output:
Medium

Input:
Love the sofa! It would be nice if it came in more colors.
Output:
Low

Input:
I've contacted support three times and nobody has responded.
Output:
High

Input:
The instructions could be a little clearer.
Output:
Low



# TASK 5 (Confidence):

Determine your confidence in the primary classification of this customer feedback.
Confidence measures how clearly the customer expresses a single, identifiable topic.

# DECISION RULES:
- Confidence measures the certainty of your classification, not the customer's certainty.
- Do not lower confidence simply because the customer expresses multiple opinions if one classification is clearly dominant.
- Do lower confidence when multiple classifications are equally supported.
- Short feedback should not automatically receive a low confidence score. For example, "The sofa arrived damaged." should receive a very high confidence.
- Long feedback should not automatically receive a high confidence score. Length does not imply clarity.
- Do not consider whether the feedback is positive or negative.
- Do not consider the severity or business impact of the issue.
- Base the score solely on the clarity and specificity of the evidence in the feedback.

# CONFIDENCE LEVELS
Return an integer from 0 to 1 representing your confidence that the primary classification is correct.

1
- The feedback clearly expresses a single, unmistakable classification.
- There are no competing interpretations.

0.90–0.99
- The classification is very clear.
- Minor ambiguity exists, but it does not materially affect the decision.

0.75–0.89
- One classification is the best fit, but one or two alternatives are plausible.
- Some interpretation is required.

0.50–0.74
- Multiple classifications are reasonably supported.
- The selected classification is only moderately more likely than the alternatives.

0.25–0.49
- The feedback is vague, incomplete, or contains conflicting information.
- Several classifications are equally plausible.

0.1–0.24
- There is insufficient information to confidently determine a classification.
- The selection is largely speculative.

0
- The feedback contains no information relevant to the requested classification.
- A classification cannot reasonably be determined.



# TASK 6 (Summary):

Summarize the customer feedback in one sentence by capturing only the
business-relevant information.
The summary should help a business quickly understand what the customer
experienced, what worked well, what did not, and any requested improvements.

# DECISION RULES:
- Write a concise summary of one sentence, for longer, complex feedback use two
 sentences.
- Focus on the key experience rather than conversational details.
- Write objectively from a business perspective rather than from the customer's perspective.
- Emphasize if the customer refers to an ongoing and yet unresolved issue.
- Include any requested action or desired outcome if stated.
- If multiple issues are mentioned, prioritize the issue with the greatest impact on the customer's experience.

# EXAMPLES:
Input:
Returning my order was simple, but waiting for the refund was frustrating.
Output:
Customer found returns easy but refund timing reduced satisfaction.

Input:
I still don't know if my issue has been resolved because nobody followed up with me.
Output:
Lack of follow-up left the customer uncertain whether the issue was resolved.

# OUTPUT:
Return only the summary.
